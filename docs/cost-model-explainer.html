3 · The central design decision

Two streams, and neither can create money

Cost reaches a patient by exactly one route. Each route has its own limit, which is why the total can never grow on the way down.

A pool belongs to one stream only, and the system works out which: a pool that pays for a resource is consumption, every other pool is allocated by rule (CR-4).

4 · How a run works

Eleven stages, in strict order

Each stage reads only from the stages before it and records the money that went in and came out. Where the two stop matching is where money went missing. Stages with a teal underline move money.

6 · Support services

Reciprocal allocation, one iteration at a time

IT serves Facilities and Facilities serves IT. Neither can close first, so their balances are passed back and forth until what circulates is below a halala. Every iteration is a number an auditor can follow. Production centres keep what they receive.

After the reciprocal pass, everything else is stepped down in service order, then each operating centre's balance goes to its activities by driver. A zero driver volume sends the balance to NO_DRIVER_VOLUME, never to a made-up denominator.

7 · Explaining the difference

Rate, quantity and mix always add up to actual minus standard

The overlap between price and practice is kept as its own term, so the answer to "was it price or practice?" never depends on a convention. Try it with nursing on a caesarean section.

Rate effect(actual rate − standard rate) × standard qty
Quantity effect(actual qty − standard qty) × standard rate
Mix effect(Δ rate) × (Δ qty)
Totalactual − standard

Rate belongs to procurement and payroll. Quantity belongs to clinical practice and pathways. Positive numbers are over standard.

9 · How the software is built

Architecture: Angular, .NET 8, SQL Server
